10 Benefits of Using Puppets in the Classroom

Puppets are one of those classroom tools that look simple but do something remarkable: they make children braver. I have watched the quietest kids in a room suddenly find their voice the moment a puppet enters the conversation. Here is what the research says, and ten ways puppets can transform your classroom.

What the Research Says

Puppets allow children to engage in imaginative play that differs from dolls and other figures. While using puppets, children are able to project their own emotions onto the puppet. Research suggests that children view puppets more like a peer than an adult, which makes them more likely to explain their ideas to a puppet than to the adult operating it.

A 2015 study investigated the learning impact of puppets in a kindergarten classroom and found a tremendous effect on children's education. The puppet helped to motivate students, created an emotional relationship with them and added a playful mood to the learning environment (Ronit, 2015). These are not small gains.

10 Benefits of Using Puppets in the Classroom

1. Puppets capture children's attention immediately

When a puppet appears, the room shifts. Young children in particular are drawn in instantly. Puppets are one of the most reliable tools for getting a group settled and focused, without raising your voice or waiting for quiet.

2. Puppets help children act out everyday scenarios

From taking turns to navigating a disagreement, puppets give children a safe distance to explore situations they encounter every day. Acting out a scenario with a puppet feels playful, not like a lesson, and that is exactly why it works.

3. Puppets allow children to express emotions safely

Children can project feelings onto a puppet that they may not yet have the language or confidence to express themselves. The puppet becomes a bridge. This is especially powerful for children who are going through something difficult at home or struggling to name what they feel.

4. Puppets support storytelling, singing and dancing

As a visual and tactile prop, puppets bring stories to life in a way that pictures alone cannot. Finger puppets in particular allow little ones to transform into any character they want, making literacy and music activities richer and more memorable.

5. Puppets help shy students speak in front of the class

This is one of my favourite benefits. When a shy child speaks through a puppet, it is the puppet answering, not them. That tiny shift in perspective can be enough to unlock a child who would otherwise stay silent. Research confirms it: puppets give timid students a way in (Belfiore, 2016).

6. Puppets build oral speaking and language skills

Children can practice explaining a concept to a puppet, retell a story, or describe what they see. These are rich oral language opportunities that feel like play. Teachers can also use puppets to model pronunciation and vocabulary in context.

7. Puppets develop literacy skills

Students can read to a puppet or read as the puppet. Both approaches give children a reason to engage with text and practice fluency in a low-pressure way. Reading to a puppet removes the anxiety of reading in front of peers.

8. Puppets help children retain and retell information

When children teach the puppet what they have just learned, they consolidate understanding. The puppet becomes a willing and endlessly patient listener. This technique works across subjects and age groups.

9. Puppets check for understanding in a playful way

After covering a concept, let the puppet be confused and have children correct it. Or ask the puppet a question and have students help it answer. This gives you an instant read on comprehension without putting any child on the spot.

10. Puppets teach conflict resolution and empathy

Use puppets to act out a disagreement and then invite children to suggest how the puppets could resolve it. This is one of the most powerful tools for teaching kindness and empathy, because children are problem-solving for someone else, which makes it feel approachable and safe.

Little ones need ways to express their emotions, and puppets provide a gentle, playful path in. Whether you use them for morning meeting, literacy, or a tricky conversation about feelings, the impact is real.

References

Belfiore, C. (2016). Puppets talk, children listen. Retrieved from teachmag.com/archives/5618.

Ronit, R., & Tzuriel, D. (2015). I teach better with the puppet: use of puppet as a mediating tool in kindergarten education. American Journal of Educational Research, 3(3), 356-365.
